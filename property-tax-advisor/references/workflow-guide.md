# End-to-end workflow guide

## Workflow 1: Consumer checks a residential Arnona bill

### Inputs

- Arnona bill for the current period.
- Annual municipal Arnona order.
- Apartment size from bill, Tabu extract, purchase contract, or rental agreement.
- Eligibility documents for discounts.

### Steps

1. Record municipality, account number, asset number, period, due date, area, zone, usage, rate, and discounts.
2. Open the current annual Arnona order.
3. Match the street and zone.
4. Match the residential classification and measurement rule.
5. Recalculate annual and period charges.
6. Compare the bill to the recalculation.
7. Check discount eligibility and missing documents.
8. Decide action:
   - Pay if the charge is consistent and no discount is missing.
   - Submit a discount form if only eligibility is missing.
   - Request measurement if the area appears wrong.
   - File an objection if holder, area, classification, zone, or exemption is wrong.

### Output

```text
Finding:
Estimated overcharge:
Main reason:
Documents attached:
Deadline:
Recommended next step:
```

## Workflow 2: Freelancer working from home

### Inputs

- Arnona bill before and after reclassification.
- Lease and home-office description.
- Photos showing actual use.
- Floor plan with work area marked.
- Business activity evidence.

### Steps

1. Determine whether the entire apartment, part of it, or no part is charged as business.
2. Identify business signs, customer visits, separate entrance, employees, stock, clinic equipment, or production activity.
3. Review the municipal order for mixed-use assets and home-based activity.
4. Calculate three scenarios:
   - All residential.
   - Split residential/business by measured area.
   - All business.
5. Compare annual impact.
6. Prepare request to classify only the actual business area if supported by facts.
7. Avoid claiming “no business use” if evidence clearly shows a dedicated business area.

### Example

```text
Apartment: 72 sqm
Work room: 10 sqm
Residential rate sample: ₪95 per sqm
Office rate sample: ₪330 per sqm

All residential: 72 × 95 = ₪6,840
All office: 72 × 330 = ₪23,760
Split: 62 × 95 + 10 × 330 = ₪9,190
Potential annual gap between all-office and split: ₪14,570
```

## Workflow 3: Small store receives wrong classification

### Inputs

- Current and previous tenant information.
- Lease and possession date.
- Business license category.
- Photos of inventory and layout.
- Invoices or POS reports showing activity.
- Municipal classification definition.

### Steps

1. Confirm possession date and holder update.
2. Compare current activity to the billed classification.
3. Check whether the classification belongs to the prior tenant.
4. Prepare a timeline:
   - Contract signed.
   - Possession received.
   - Business opened.
   - Notice received.
5. Attach photos and business license evidence.
6. Request correction from the possession date.
7. Ask for reversal of late fees caused by the incorrect classification if appropriate.

## Workflow 4: New buyer estimates purchase tax

### Inputs

- Contract date in DD/MM/YYYY.
- Purchase price.
- Asset type.
- Buyer status.
- Existing property interests of buyer, spouse, and minor children.
- New immigrant/disability/other relief documents if relevant.

### Steps

1. Determine whether the asset is a residential apartment, land, commercial asset, or real estate association right.
2. Determine buyer profile.
3. Retrieve current Israel Tax Authority brackets for the contract date.
4. Calculate tax by marginal bracket.
5. Check declaration and payment deadlines.
6. Preserve source and calculation table.
7. Escalate if the family unit owns partial interests or there is a complex trust, company, gift, inheritance, or combination transaction.

### Output table

| Bracket | Amount taxed | Rate | Tax |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% bracket | ₪X | 0% | ₪0 |
| 3.5% bracket | ₪Y | 3.5% | ₪Y × 3.5% |
| 5% bracket | ₪Z | 5% | ₪Z × 5% |

## Workflow 5: Seller checks betterment levy exposure

### Inputs

- Draft sale agreement or sale plan.
- Local committee demand or pre-ruling.
- Plan numbers affecting the property.
- Building permits and approved rights.
- Appraiser report if available.

### Steps

1. Identify the realization event: sale, permit, or use.
2. Identify the plan, relief, or use change creating betterment.
3. Confirm valuation date and ownership share.
4. Calculate rough levy: planning uplift × 50%.
5. Check exemptions, prior payments, statutory limits, and appraiser dispute routes.
6. Compare committee appraiser assumptions to actual market constraints.
7. Consider counter-appraisal or statutory review route.
8. Reserve funds in the sale transaction if timing is uncertain.

## Workflow 6: Mas Rechush direct-damage claim

### Inputs

- Incident date and location.
- Photos and videos.
- Ownership or tenancy proof.
- Emergency service confirmations.
- Repair invoices and quotes.
- Insurance correspondence.

### Steps

1. Ensure safety and obtain emergency repairs when necessary.
2. Photograph every damaged area before full repair.
3. Create an itemized damage list.
4. Preserve damaged items until inspection if safe.
5. Submit through the Tax Authority compensation route.
6. Record claim number and submission date.
7. Reply promptly to requests for appraiser inspection or missing documents.
8. Keep copies of all invoices and communications.

### Red flags

- Damage repaired with no photos.
- Missing proof of connection to the event.
- Claim filed under the wrong track.
- Business interruption claim confused with direct physical damage.
- Duplicate claim to insurer and state fund without disclosure.

## Workflow 7: Vacant-property exemption

### Inputs

- Property status and dates.
- Photos showing empty property.
- Utility consumption.
- Lease termination or possession documents.
- Previous use of vacancy exemption.

### Steps

1. Check whether the local authority offers a vacant-property exemption and for how long.
2. Confirm the property is empty of people and chattels.
3. Verify whether the exemption is one-time or limited per owner/property.
4. Submit the official form before or immediately after vacancy starts.
5. Request inspection if required.
6. Track approval period and end date.
7. Avoid claiming vacancy after reoccupation.

## Workflow 8: Municipal measurement challenge

### Inputs

- Municipal measurement report.
- Independent measurement or floor plan.
- Annual order measurement clause.
- Photos of disputed spaces.
- Tabu or sale plan.

### Steps

1. Determine exactly what the municipal order includes in taxable area.
2. Mark differences: walls, common areas, balconies, parking, storage, basement, gallery, yard.
3. Do not compare only to Tabu net area.
4. Ask municipality for measurement sketch and method.
5. Submit a professional measurement when the discrepancy is material.
6. Calculate refund or future reduction.
7. File objection if the authority refuses correction and deadline remains open.

## Workflow 9: Business sign charge

### Inputs

- Sign photos.
- Dimensions in cm.
- Installation date.
- Business location.
- Municipal sign bylaw.
- Prior permit or license correspondence.

### Steps

1. Measure total sign area.
2. Classify sign type: illuminated, wall, window, temporary, shared, directional.
3. Check whether the bylaw charges by area, side, illumination, zone, or duration.
4. Verify whether the charge period matches installation date.
5. Request correction for removed or incorrectly measured signs.
6. Separate sign tax from Arnona; use the correct municipal procedure.

## Workflow 10: Debt collection and arrears

### Inputs

- Original bill.
- Demand letters.
- Payment history.
- Holder-change documents.
- Objection or discount submissions.
- Collection-fee breakdown.

### Steps

1. Separate principal, linkage, interest, legal fees, collection fees, and current charges.
2. Check whether the debt belongs to the correct holder and period.
3. Verify whether an objection, appeal, discount, or correction request paused or affected collection.
4. Request account ledger.
5. Pay undisputed current charges when possible to reduce enforcement risk.
6. Challenge collection costs separately if the principal was wrong or notice was defective.
