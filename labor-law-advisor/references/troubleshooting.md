# Troubleshooting

Use this file when a user describes a confusing, contradictory, or high-risk labor-law situation.

## Rapid triage table

| User symptom | Possible issue | Questions to ask | Immediate action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salary looks legal but hourly rate is low | Monthly salary divided by too many hours | What are regular hours and unpaid breaks? | Recalculate hourly base and overtime |
| Payslip has no hours | Recordkeeping issue | Is the worker monthly or hourly? Is there a time report? | Request attendance records |
| "Global overtime" appears every month | Invalid or underfunded overtime component | Is it separate and reasonable? Are actual hours tracked? | Compare actual overtime to component |
| Employer calls worker freelancer | Misclassification | Who controls schedule, tools, clients, substitutes? | Apply employee-status checklist |
| No pension deposits | Pension start-date violation | Did worker have active pension before hire? | Check fund reports and start rule |
| Pension exists but no severance release | Section 14/release document issue | Is there Form 161 and release letter? | Request documents in writing |

## Minimum wage troubleshooting

### Pay includes reimbursements

Do not count travel reimbursement, expense refunds, one-time gifts, or conditional bonuses as base wage unless a specific legal rule permits it. Compare statutory minimum to ordinary wage for regular work.

### Worker is paid per task

Convert to actual working time. If task pay divided by hours falls below hourly minimum, flag a likely shortfall. Piece-rate structures still need wage-floor compliance.

### Worker is "training"

Training time required by the employer can be work time. Do not assume training can be unpaid.

### Deductions reduce pay

Create a deduction table:

| Deduction | Amount | Written consent? | Legal basis? | Reduces below minimum? |
|---|---:|---|---|---|

If the legal basis is unclear, flag for payroll or legal review.

## Overtime troubleshooting

### Missing attendance records

Use alternative evidence:

- Shift schedules.
- WhatsApp messages.
- Calendar invitations.
- Security entry logs.
- POS/cash-register logs.
- GPS/work-app data.
- Emails sent during late hours.
- Witnesses.

### Monthly salary with no hourly rate

Estimate hourly value from salary and normal monthly hours, then label the result as an estimate. Do not treat the estimate as final if the contract or payroll uses a different lawful basis.

### Manager exemption claimed

Check actual role, not title:

- Can the worker hire, fire, discipline, or set policy?
- Does the worker control budget?
- Is the salary high enough to reflect trust and seniority?
- Are hours genuinely not trackable?
- Is the role central management or ordinary execution?

If answers are mostly no, do not accept the exemption without legal review.

## Severance troubleshooting

### Resignation after worsening conditions

Check:

1. What changed?
2. When did it change?
3. Did the employee object and give the employer a chance to fix it?
4. Is there written evidence?
5. Did resignation follow within a reasonable time?

### Section 14 unclear

Request:

- Employment agreement.
- Pension enrollment forms.
- Extension order or collective agreement.
- Fund statement showing severance component.
- Employer release letter.
- Form 161.

Do not assume Section 14 covers years before deposits began.

### Variable pay

Gather at least 12 months of pay data when material. Identify fixed components, commissions, shift premiums, bonuses, and reimbursements. Use a payroll specialist or attorney for large sums.

## Parental-rights troubleshooting

### Fixed-term contract not renewed

Non-renewal can be treated like dismissal in protected-status cases. Check permit requirement before relying on contract end date.

### Reduction in hours instead of dismissal

A reduction in scope or income can also require a permit or create discrimination risk. Treat it as a protected employment action.

### Employee did not disclose pregnancy immediately

Protections may still apply depending on timing and employer knowledge. Do not advise retaliation for late disclosure.

## Collective agreement troubleshooting

### Agreement cannot be found

Search by:

- Employer legal name in Hebrew and English.
- Employer association.
- Sector.
- Worksite or client site.
- Union or committee name.
- Old company name after merger.

### Several sources conflict

Apply this order:

1. Mandatory statutory minimum.
2. Extension order or collective agreement if more favorable.
3. Personal contract if more favorable.
4. Workplace custom if proven and more favorable.

Do not use a less favorable contract term to waive a statutory right.

## Escalation triggers

Escalate to a labor-law attorney, union representative, Ministry of Labor, or Regional Labor Court information desk when:

- Pregnancy, fertility, birth, parenting, adoption, or surrogacy protections are involved.
- Dismissal or reduction may require a permit.
- Harassment, discrimination, retaliation, violence, or threats appear.
- Underpayment spans many employees or large sums.
- Worker classification is disputed.
- Collective agreement coverage is unclear and material.
- A deadline is near.
- Documents appear falsified.
