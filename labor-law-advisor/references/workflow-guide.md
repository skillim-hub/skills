# Workflow Guide

Use these workflows to produce consistent, practical guidance for Israeli employees, small businesses, freelancers, and consumers.

## Workflow 1: Minimum wage audit for a small business

### Goal
Check whether a worker is paid at least the statutory minimum and identify correction steps.

### Inputs
Pay month, monthly salary or hourly wage, position percentage or regular hours, payslip lines, attendance report, worker category, and sector.

### Steps
1. Set the effective date.
2. Verify the current minimum wage from an official source.
3. Separate base pay from reimbursements, overtime, bonuses, and deductions.
4. For monthly work, calculate `minimum_monthly × position_fraction`.
5. For hourly work, calculate `minimum_hourly × regular_hours`.
6. Compare actual base pay to required base pay.
7. Review deductions that reduce pay unlawfully.
8. Calculate arrears for each pay period.
9. Check pension, vacation, sick leave, convalescence, and travel if underpayment suggests broader errors.
10. Produce a correction table and document the source rate.

## Workflow 2: Overtime claim from attendance logs

### Inputs
Hourly rate or monthly salary converted to hourly value, start/end times, breaks, workweek type, night work, weekly rest, holidays, permits, and payslips.

### Steps
1. Normalize dates to DD-MM-YYYY.
2. Remove unpaid breaks only when the worker was actually free.
3. Mark the daily threshold.
4. Calculate the first two overtime hours at 125%.
5. Calculate later overtime at 150%.
6. Check weekly threshold without double counting.
7. Mark weekly rest and holiday work separately.
8. Compare with payslip overtime lines.
9. Prepare a day-by-day table.
10. Flag uncertainty for night work, global overtime, managerial exemptions, or permits.

## Workflow 3: Severance and pension release after termination

### Inputs
Start/end dates, dismissal/resignation reason, last monthly salary, fixed additions, variable pay history, pension reports, Section 14 clause, termination letter, hearing documents.

### Steps
1. Confirm continuity and one-year threshold.
2. Identify dismissal or resignation treated as dismissal.
3. Calculate tenure.
4. Estimate statutory severance using last monthly salary × tenure.
5. Check whether Section 14 covers the full period and rate.
6. Compare fund severance balance with statutory estimate.
7. Identify possible top-up.
8. Request Form 161, fund release letters, and final payslip.
9. Check payment deadline.
10. Escalate if funds are withheld or protected status applies.

## Workflow 4: Pregnancy or parental-rights termination risk

### Inputs
Protected status, tenure, employer knowledge date, proposed action, business reason, prior records, and documents.

### Steps
1. Confirm status and exact dates.
2. Check whether a Ministry of Labor permit is required.
3. If permit may be required, stop implementation.
4. Preserve neutral business documentation.
5. Avoid pressure to resign.
6. Conduct a hearing only in a lawful process.
7. Provide written decisions and avoid discriminatory reasoning.
8. Track return-to-work protections.
9. Refer to counsel before irreversible action.
10. Document accommodations and schedule requests.

### Red flags
"The role is too demanding now", "reduce hours until after birth", "let the contract expire because of maternity leave", "ask for resignation", or "delete messages".

## Workflow 5: Histadrut or collective agreement coverage check

### Inputs
Employer legal name, workplace site, sector, job title, union-dues line, study-fund line, employment agreement, committee communication.

### Steps
1. Search for a workplace collective agreement.
2. Search for a sectoral extension order.
3. Check coverage clauses and exclusions.
4. Check effective dates and renewal status.
5. Compare terms with contract and statutory minimums.
6. Identify higher entitlements.
7. Calculate arrears if the statutory minimum was used instead of a collective floor.
8. Preserve source documents.
9. Suggest contacting the worker committee, Histadrut branch, payroll, or counsel as appropriate.
10. Do not assume union membership is required for every extended right.

## Workflow 6: Freelancer classified as invoice worker

### Inputs
Service agreement, invoices, schedule, reporting line, tools, work location, number of clients, duration, exclusivity, substitute rights, integration.

### Steps
1. Apply employee-status indicators.
2. Identify economic dependence and control.
3. Check whether the business used the person like staff.
4. Estimate exposure: minimum wage, overtime, vacation, sick leave, pension, severance, notice.
5. Compare contractor compensation to hypothetical employee cost.
6. Note that offset arguments are fact-sensitive; do not assume a full offset.
7. Recommend settlement or legal review for material exposure.
8. Align future contracts with the actual working reality.

## Workflow 7: Consumer hiring a household worker or caregiver

1. Decide whether the consumer is the direct employer or only pays an agency.
2. If direct employer, check minimum wage, pension, vacation, sick leave, convalescence, and severance.
3. Track hours and payments.
4. Register and report where required.
5. Keep written terms even for household work.
6. Review termination notice and severance before ending the arrangement.

## Workflow 8: Payroll launch checklist for a new Israeli small business

1. Prepare written employment terms.
2. Choose a time-tracking method.
3. Configure payroll for daily overtime and 42-hour weekly checks.
4. Add a minimum wage rate table and review date.
5. Configure pension start rules.
6. Track vacation, sick leave, convalescence, holidays, travel, and deductions.
7. Add a process for pregnancy, fertility, and parenting protected status.
8. Build a termination workflow: hearing, notice, final payslip, Form 161, fund release.
9. Identify sectoral extension orders before first payroll.
10. Keep records for audit and dispute defense.
