# Troubleshooting Guide

## Principles

Separate facts from assumptions. Use current official rates. Treat written policy and actual practice as separate evidence. Rank uncertainty rather than forcing a final answer. Escalate critical and high-risk findings.

## Worker might be contractor or employee

Ask who controls hours, location, substitution, tools, clients, leave, discipline, price, profit/loss, and business integration. Score each indicator. If several point toward employee status, provide a high-risk misclassification finding even when invoices exist.

## Minimum wage result seems wrong

Common causes: wrong pay-period rate, monthly salary compared to hourly minimum without hours conversion, unpaid training or closing time excluded, tips treated as guaranteed base pay, deductions ignored, part-time ratio estimated incorrectly, youth/adult rate confused.

Fix: verify official rate, build a compensable-hours list, exclude illegal deductions from wage satisfaction, compare guaranteed pay to required amount, and mark variable-pay assumptions.

## Overtime calculation double-counts hours

Common causes: daily and weekly overtime both applied to the same hour, rest-day work mixed with weekday overtime, night-work rules ignored, global overtime counted without legal review.

Fix: build a daily ledger with regular hours, daily overtime, rest-day work, night work, paid amount, and notes. Reconcile weekly totals after daily categorization.

## Pension start date is unclear

Common causes: prior active pension status unknown, employee did not submit fund details, probation treated as no-pension period, contributions started without retroactive amounts.

Fix: ask for proof of active arrangement before start date; compare first deposit date, retroactive deposit, employer pension component, employee component, and severance component.

## Section 14 is mentioned but unclear

Common causes: clause exists without a valid arrangement, severance contributions below required level, full waiver assumed from partial deposits, fund-release terms conflict with policy.

Fix: flag medium or high risk and request payroll/legal verification. Do not state that severance exposure is eliminated unless deposits and written terms are verified.

## Termination already happened

Common causes: hearing skipped, hearing invitation lacked reasons, final decision made before hearing, protected status discovered late, final wage or notice withheld.

Fix: create a remedial timeline, preserve records, calculate undisputed final payments, and escalate before sending any release or settlement proposal.

## Protected-status facts are sensitive

Common causes: manager comments link action to pregnancy, fertility treatment, parenthood, disability, reserve duty, age, religion, nationality, or complaint activity.

Fix: pause adverse action, limit access to sensitive details, document objective reasons, and escalate.

## Sector expansion order may apply

Common causes: cleaning, guarding, construction, hospitality, transport, caregiving, or public-contractor work; service contractor at a client site; role inside a sector-specific arrangement.

Fix: ask for exact sector, client site, role, employer identity, and service agreement. Verify the relevant expansion order.

## Hebrew terminology

Use שכר מינימום, שעות נוספות, מנוחה שבועית, הודעה מוקדמת, פיצויי פיטורים, פנסיה חובה, דמי הבראה, החזר נסיעות, תלוש שכר, יחסי עובד-מעסיק, שימוע, מצב מוגן.

## Data minimization

Redact ID numbers, bank account details, pension account identifiers, medical documents beyond minimal status facts, child names, immigration documents, home addresses, and unrelated disciplinary records.
