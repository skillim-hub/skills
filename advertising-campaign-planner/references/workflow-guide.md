# Workflow Guide

## Workflow 1: Local service lead generation

Use for accountants, repair services, lawyers, clinics, tutors, photographers, consultants, and local trades.

1. Define one primary conversion: call, WhatsApp, booking, or form.
2. Split geography into core service area and expansion area.
3. Build high-intent Hebrew keywords; add Arabic/Russian/English only when service exists in that language.
4. Add negative keywords: jobs, free, DIY, salary, supplier, unrelated cities, and irrelevant services.
5. Create a landing page with service area, offer, proof, phone/WhatsApp/form, privacy notice, accessibility basics, and professional disclosures when relevant.
6. Allocate 55% search, 20% social prospecting, 15% retargeting, 10% creative/testing when budget permits.
7. Track qualified lead rate, answer rate, appointment rate, close rate, and break-even CPL.
8. Review after 3, 7, and 14 days.

## Workflow 2: Ecommerce sales

1. Validate margin, shipping, returns, VAT display, stock, and payment path.
2. Segment products into hero item, bundle, repeat-purchase item, and high-margin add-on.
3. Track purchase value and gross profit, not only revenue.
4. Create Hebrew copy and native Arabic/Russian variants only when fulfillment and support can serve them.
5. Allocate 35% search/catalog, 35% social prospecting, 20% retargeting, 10% creative.
6. Show discount amount, valid dates in DD/MM/YYYY, minimum purchase, delivery exclusions, return/cancellation policy, and material terms near the CTA.
7. Review CPA, gross-profit ROAS, refund rate, delivery complaints, and new-vs-returning split.

## Workflow 3: Multilingual city campaign

1. Confirm language service capability.
2. Map languages to practical regions and customer journeys.
3. Separate campaigns or ad sets by language when budget allows.
4. Use native landing sections and contact routing.
5. Track language in CRM.
6. Judge by qualified lead rate and close rate, not only CPL.
7. Stop language variants that produce leads the business cannot serve.

## Workflow 4: Freelancer and consultant B2B

1. Define buyer role: owner, finance manager, operations manager, founder, HR, or product lead.
2. Decide whether search demand exists.
3. Use Google Search for active demand and LinkedIn/Meta for problem-aware audiences where relevant.
4. Offer an audit, diagnostic call, checklist, calculator, or scoped consultation.
5. Add qualification fields: company size, need, timing, and budget range.
6. Calculate break-even CPL from close rate and project margin.
7. Follow up within one business day.
8. Measure proposal rate and signed-client rate.

## Workflow 5: Regulated or sensitive campaign

1. Identify claim, license, audience, and data risks.
2. Separate factual service information from promotional claims.
3. Remove guarantees, cure promises, tax-saving promises, and unsupported superlatives.
4. Document substantiation for claims.
5. Review ad, landing page, lead form, and follow-up messages together.
6. Add manual approval before launch.
7. Use conservative targeting and frequency.

## Workflow 6: No-website campaign

1. Choose platform lead form, WhatsApp, phone call, or booking page.
2. Add privacy and consent text where the platform permits.
3. Keep fields minimal but include one quality filter.
4. Track leads in a sheet or CRM.
5. Add a simple landing page when the service is high-consideration.
6. Avoid remarketing until privacy setup is reviewed.

## Workflow 7: 14-day optimization routine

- Day 0: verify tracking, forms, phone, WhatsApp, UTM, budget caps, and moderation.
- Day 3: review disapprovals, search terms, comments, landing errors, and spend pacing.
- Day 7: compare CPL/CPA with break-even; pause weak creative, audiences, or keywords.
- Day 14: scale 15%–30%, hold, pivot the offer, or stop according to qualified lead and gross-profit data.
