# End-to-end document workflows

Use these workflows to produce consistent Israeli contract drafts from intake to delivery.

## Workflow 1 — Freelance service agreement for a small business

### Intake

Collect provider and customer details, service description, deliverables, milestones, fee, VAT treatment, payment schedule, expenses, IP expectation, confidentiality needs, termination period, and personal-data status.

### Decision points

1. If the provider is an individual working fixed hours under the customer's management, flag worker-classification risk.
2. If the deliverables include creative work, choose assignment or license.
3. If payment is in installments, define each milestone and invoice date.
4. If scope may expand, add change-request procedure.

### Draft sections

1. Title and parties.
2. Background.
3. Services and deliverables.
4. Customer obligations.
5. Consideration and tax documents.
6. Delivery, review, acceptance, and changes.
7. IP.
8. Confidentiality and privacy.
9. Warranties.
10. Liability.
11. Termination.
12. Disputes and law.
13. Notices and signature blocks.
14. Statement of work appendix.

### Example output note

> The draft assumes the provider is an independent business. Facts suggesting fixed hours, exclusivity, direct supervision, or integration into the customer's workforce require employment-law review.

## Workflow 2 — Consumer service agreement

### Intake

Collect business name, business number, address, customer-service contact, consumer name, service description, exact price and VAT status, date, location, arrival window, cancellation/rescheduling rules, warranty, parts/materials, payment method, and whether the transaction is online, by phone, at home, or in-store.

### Decision points

1. If sale is remote or at consumer's home, add cancellation-right review.
2. If product/service has exceptions to cancellation, flag professional review.
3. If warranty is offered, distinguish commercial warranty from mandatory rights.
4. If price includes VAT, write it clearly.

### Draft sections

1. Service order / agreement.
2. Service details.
3. Price and payment.
4. Scheduling and access.
5. Cancellation and rescheduling.
6. Parts and warranty.
7. Liability and mandatory rights.
8. Privacy and communications.
9. Law and dispute resolution.
10. Signature/approval.

### Anti-pattern removal

Replace "no refunds in any case" with:

> Nothing in this agreement derogates from mandatory rights available to the customer under applicable law. Cancellation fees, if any, apply only to the extent permitted by law and according to the circumstances of the cancellation.

## Workflow 3 — NDA for business discussions

### Intake

Collect disclosing and receiving parties, mutual or one-way structure, purpose, confidential-information categories, discussion period, confidentiality period, permitted recipients, return/destruction procedure, exceptions, remedies, governing law, and venue.

### Decision points

1. Use mutual NDA when both sides disclose material non-public information.
2. Avoid non-compete unless the business facts justify a separate restrictive covenant.
3. Add residual knowledge only after review because it can weaken confidentiality.
4. If personal data is shared, add privacy terms.

### Draft sections

1. Parties and purpose.
2. Definition of confidential information.
3. Obligations.
4. Exclusions.
5. Permitted disclosures.
6. Protection measures.
7. Return/destruction.
8. No license / no obligation to transact.
9. Remedies.
10. Term.
11. Law and venue.
12. Signatures.

## Workflow 4 — Sale or supply of goods

### Intake

Collect seller and buyer details, goods description, model, quantity, serial numbers if any, price, VAT, payment schedule, delivery place/date, inspection period, warranty/conformity, risk and title transfer, installation/training, returns, and purchase order process.

### Decision points

1. For B2C, apply consumer workflow.
2. For custom goods, specify drawings/specs and acceptance.
3. For recurring supply, create master terms plus purchase order mechanism.
4. For goods with safety/regulatory obligations, require compliance representation.

### Draft sections

1. Sale/purchase terms.
2. Goods and specifications.
3. Orders.
4. Price, VAT, invoice.
5. Delivery and risk.
6. Inspection and acceptance.
7. Warranty and remedies.
8. Title retention, if intended.
9. Compliance.
10. Liability.
11. Termination.
12. Disputes.

## Workflow 5 — Website or app terms for Israeli users

### Intake

Collect operator details, service description, user types, payment/subscription details, cancellation and renewals, user content, privacy-policy relation, prohibited uses, support availability, liability, warranties, and governing law.

### Decision points

1. If consumers subscribe, review cancellation and renewal requirements.
2. If minors can use the service, add guardian and privacy review.
3. If personal data is collected, create privacy policy and data-security appendix.
4. If marketplace/payment/financial services are involved, flag regulated review.

### Draft sections

1. Acceptance of terms.
2. Service description.
3. Account and eligibility.
4. Fees and subscriptions.
5. Cancellation.
6. User content and IP.
7. Privacy.
8. Prohibited conduct.
9. Availability and changes.
10. Liability.
11. Termination.
12. Law and venue.
13. Contact details.

## Workflow 6 — Contract revision and negotiation matrix

1. Identify the clause.
2. Classify issue: commercial, legal, tax/accounting, privacy, IP, consumer, litigation.
3. State risk in one sentence.
4. Provide balanced replacement language.
5. Provide fallback language.
6. Mark "must fix" only when legal or severe risk justifies it.

| Clause | Issue | Risk | Preferred wording | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payment | VAT missing | Dispute over final price | Add plus/included VAT text | Add accountant-verification placeholder |
| IP | Transfer before payment | Supplier loses leverage | Transfer after full payment | License until final payment |
| Liability | Unlimited | Uninsurable risk | Cap at fees paid in 12 months | Cap at 2x fees |
| Termination | No cure | Abrupt cancellation | 14-day cure for remediable breach | Immediate termination only for severe breach |

## Workflow 7 — Final production handoff

Provide draft agreement, schedule/appendix, risk notes outside the agreement, open issues list, signature checklist, and version label.

Version label example:

```text
Draft v1.0 — prepared for commercial review — 02/06/2026
```

Final checks:

- No branding, visual identity assets, or promotional text.
- Hebrew dates use DD/MM/YYYY.
- Amounts use ₪ and clear VAT wording.
- Statutory rights are not waived.
- Personal data terms appear when needed.
- The signer can plausibly bind the party.
- The contract is not used to disguise employment.


## Workflow 8 — Live-source verification before production use

1. Confirm the standard VAT rate and invoice-allocation rules on Tax Authority pages.
2. Confirm whether the agreement is a business contract, consumer contract, standard contract, employment contract, or collective agreement before selecting interpretation wording.
3. Confirm public registry details from data.gov.il or the Corporations Authority search, then ask the user to confirm signing authority.
4. For foreign-currency pricing, read the current Bank of Israel representative-rate source and define whether the rate is binding for the parties.
5. For personal data, map the database, role, supplier access, incident notice, and deletion/return duties before drafting the privacy schedule.
6. Preserve the verification date in the drafting notes, not in the legal text unless the date is part of the commercial bargain.
